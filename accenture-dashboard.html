Company snapshot

The scale player in technology services: ~799,000 people, $69.7B of FY2025 revenue, and $80.6B of new bookings. It has partnered with 195 of its top 200 clients for ten years or more, which makes it the default incumbent inside most large enterprises rather than a challenger.

Offering: Strategy & Consulting, Technology, Operations and Song, delivered through industry groups and a large offshore delivery network. Its AI delivery runs on internal platforms such as GenWizard, and it reports generative and agentic AI as a separate revenue line.

HQ: Dublin, Ireland

05

Sentiment

Customer sentiment
Client retention is the strongest signal in the file — 195 of the top 200 clients have been with the firm for a decade or more, and FY2025 produced a record 129 quarterly bookings above $100M. The recurring criticism is the one that follows every large SI: pyramid staffing means the people who win the work are not always the people who deliver it, and buyers who don't write delivery seniority into the contract tend to be disappointed.
Market & analyst sentiment
What supports the story
  • Only firm in this peer set reporting AI as a disclosed revenue line — $2.7B in FY25, tripled year over year
  • $80.6B of FY2025 new bookings with a book-to-bill above 1, and management claiming market-share gains at more than 5x the market
  • Ecosystem breadth: a top-tier partner to every major cloud and enterprise software vendor, which matters when the client's stack is already fragmented
  • Delivery capacity is genuinely hard to replicate — 799,000 people is a structural moat against boutique competitors
What to pressure-test
  • Growth has slowed materially: FY2026 guidance of 2–5% local currency against 7% delivered in FY2025
  • A six-month business optimisation programme took a $615M charge in Q4 FY2025, including $344M of severance — roughly 22,000 roles were cut for staff deemed non-reskillable
  • Federal business (AFS) is a drag on FY2026 growth, worth about 1.5 points
  • The AI revenue line is impressive in absolute terms but is still under 4% of total revenue — the core business is still classic systems integration
  • Attrition at 14–15% annualised makes continuity of named delivery staff a real contracting risk
Employee sentiment
Accenture's own January 2026 Pulse of Change survey found worker confidence in AI job security had fallen to 48%, down 11 points from summer 2025, and that only 40% of employees felt ready for AI-augmented work. Voluntary attrition rose to 14% in FY2025 with Q4 annualised at 15%. Headcount nonetheless recovered from 779,000 to about 799,000 by Q3 FY2026, which points to aggressive hiring in AI and cloud roles alongside the cuts elsewhere — a workforce being replaced rather than shrunk.
