Company snapshot

Azure crossed $100B of annual revenue for the first time in FY2026 and accelerated to 43% growth in Q4 rather than plateauing. Microsoft 365 Copilot passed 30 million paid seats, doubling in two quarters, against a commercial base of roughly 464 million seats — so penetration is still only about 6.5%.

Offering: Azure cloud and AI infrastructure, Microsoft 365 and the Copilot family, Dynamics 365 for ERP and CRM, GitHub, Fabric for data, and the security portfolio. Nadella has stated Microsoft holds royalty-free frontier model IP rights through 2032.

Where it sits: Microsoft is the default enterprise AI route for most organisations because the commercial relationship already exists — Copilot lands inside an enterprise agreement rather than requiring a new vendor selection. That is genuinely important for a portfolio programme: it is the lowest-friction path to individual productivity across PortCos, which is one of the three opportunity areas in the reference model. Two numbers deserve attention in any negotiation. Copilot penetration at roughly 6.5% of eligible seats means Microsoft has enormous incentive to discount for volume commitments. And commercial remaining performance obligations of $678B, up 84% — still up 25% excluding OpenAI — mean the demand is broad rather than concentrated in one counterparty.

05

Sentiment

Customer sentiment
The Copilot seat data is the clearest read available and it is strong — doubling in two quarters is the fastest growth since launch. The honest counterpoint is that penetration remains around 6.5%, so more than nine in ten eligible users have not converted, and Google used exactly that gap as its competitive attack line at Cloud Next 2026. Backlog growing 25% even excluding OpenAI addresses the concentration-risk objection more convincingly than any management commentary.
Market & analyst sentiment
What supports the story
  • Azure crossed $100B annually and accelerated to 43% in Q4 — acceleration, not plateau, against a strong comparable
  • Record 46.8% operating margin with operating income up 21% to $155.2B while capex nearly doubled
  • $678B commercial RPO, up 84%; excluding OpenAI it still grew 25%, which dismantles the single-counterparty argument
  • Copilot at 30 million paid seats with roughly 6.5% penetration — the upsell runway inside the existing base is enormous
What to pressure-test
  • Free cash flow fell 23% while capex hit a record, with two thirds of that spend on assets Microsoft classifies as short-lived
  • GAAP profit growth of 31% versus roughly 22% adjusted — the gap is a $3.2B Anthropic investment gain, not operating performance
  • From FY2027 Microsoft extends the useful life of data centres and office buildings from 15 to 25 years, which flatters future earnings
  • Cloud gross margin slipped to 65% from 66% in Q3 as AI workloads mix in
  • Copilot penetration of about 6.5% can be read as runway or as nine in ten eligible users declining to pay
Employee sentiment
Not disclosed in the results reviewed. The one concrete signal is roughly $900M of one-time voluntary retirement costs recorded in Q4 FY2026, which indicates a meaningful workforce reshaping alongside record profitability. No independent employer-review data was gathered in this pass.
